# The vault committee: key ceremony and signing

**Written 2026-09-15, rehearsed the same day on the ZVM's local NoM L1 (chain 70).**
How to create the committee that holds the bridge vault on testnet and mainnet, and
how its members run their signers. The decision behind it is D15 in
[`DECISIONS.md`](../spec/DECISIONS.md); the code is `internal/committee` and
`zvm committee`.

---

## What this produces

A FROST(Ed25519, SHA-512) quorum — RFC 9591 — holding an ordinary `z1…` address.
Any *t* of *n* members sign together; fewer cannot. The signature is a stock Ed25519
signature, so go-zenon, Syrius and the explorer see a normal account (verified on the
go-zenon devnet 2026-09-13, `zenon-docs/protocol/facts/threshold-signatures.md`).

**No machine ever holds the vault's private key**, including during the ceremony. The key
is generated by distributed key generation (the FROST paper's KeyGen: Pedersen DKG with a
proof of knowledge per participant — the scheme Zcash Foundation's audited `frost-core`
implements as `dkg::part1/2/3`). Each member ends with a share only they have.

**Prior art for the shape of the ceremony.** Zcash Foundation's `frost-client` runs the
same three steps with a coordinator relaying messages; the Zenon bridge's orchestrators
run a TSS key generation among pillars (`keyGenThreshold` 12 on mainnet); Serai and
Chainflip hold multi-chain vaults the same way. What they share, and what this copies, is
that the coordinator only relays, every participant verifies every message, and
participants compare a transcript out of band before anything secret is sent.

## Choosing *t* and *n*

| | Devnet rehearsal | Testnet | Mainnet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Members | 3 | 5 | 7 or more, different operators and jurisdictions |
| Threshold | 2 | 3 | more than half, and at least *n* − 2 so two lost members do not lock the vault |
| Payout depth | 6 momentums | 30 | 30, see D17 |

A threshold is a trade between theft and loss: *t* colluding members can take the vault,
*n* − *t* + 1 unavailable members freeze it. A member who loses their share no longer
reduces *n* permanently: `zvm committee reshare` (below) replaces the member set and the
threshold **without moving the vault address**, so the committee can be repaired rather
than rebuilt.

## Roles

- **Courier.** Writes `ceremony.json` and moves public files between members. Needs no
  trust: a courier that swaps a file is caught by the fingerprint comparison.
- **Members.** Each runs `zvm committee` on their own machine, keeps a passphrase, and at the
  end holds a sealed share. Air-gapped machines are appropriate for mainnet: every step
  reads and writes files, and nothing but the signer talks to a network.
- **Auditor.** Anyone. `zvm committee audit` recomputes the vault address from the public
  round-1 files.

## The ceremony

Every member uses the same `zvm` build. Check its hash against the release before starting.
Each member has a passphrase of at least 12 bytes in a file (or `ZVM_COMMITTEE_PASSPHRASE`);
it seals their state and their share with scrypt and XSalsa20-Poly1305.

**1. Parameters** (courier)

```sh
zvm committee new -l1-chain-id 1 -threshold 5 -names alice,bob,carol,dave,erin,frank,grace -out ceremony.json
```

Chain ids: **1** mainnet, **3** testnet, **70** the ZVM's devnet. The file names every member
and their identifier. Every member reads it and agrees before going on.

**2. Round 1** (each member)

```sh
zvm committee round1 -ceremony ceremony.json -id 3 -passphrase-file pass -out round1/
```

Writes the sealed state `3.state` (keep it) and the public `round1/3.round1.json`: commitments
to a random polynomial, a proof of knowledge of its constant term bound to this ceremony and
chain, and an X25519 key for receiving round 2. Send the public file to the courier, who
gives every member the full `round1/` directory.

**3. Compare the fingerprint** (all members, together)

```sh
zvm committee round2 -ceremony ceremony.json -id 3 -passphrase-file pass -round1 round1/
# transcript fingerprint: 78f2-f630-9c91-c89d-a2f8-f7a3-8152-75b5
```

Without `-confirm` it verifies every proof, checks that the package filed under your id is
the one your state made, and prints the fingerprint of the whole transcript. **Read it aloud
on a call, or compare it in person, with every other member.** This is the step that makes the
ceremony safe against the courier: round 2 encrypts to the keys in round 1, and only this
comparison proves everyone holds the same keys.

**4. Round 2** (each member)

```sh
zvm committee round2 -ceremony ceremony.json -id 3 -passphrase-file pass -round1 round1/ \
  -out round2/ -confirm 78f2-f630-9c91-c89d-a2f8-f7a3-8152-75b5
```

Writes one file per other member, `3-to-5.round2.json`, each holding your polynomial evaluated
at their identifier, encrypted and authenticated to them. The courier collects all of them.

**5. Finish** (each member)

```sh
zvm committee finish -ceremony ceremony.json -id 3 -passphrase-file pass -round1 round1/ -round2 round2/ \
  -confirm 78f2-f630-9c91-c89d-a2f8-f7a3-8152-75b5 -share share -group group.json
```

Decrypts the values addressed to you, checks each against its sender's commitments — a bad
one names the sender, and the ceremony restarts without them — and writes your sealed `share`
and the public `group.json`. It prints the vault address.

**6. Agree on the result** (all members, and the auditor)

- Every member's `group.json` must be byte-identical (`sha256sum group.json`), and every
  member reads out the address.
- `zvm committee audit -ceremony ceremony.json -round1 round1/ -group group.json` recomputes it
  from public data alone.
- Each member **deletes their `.state` file**. Only `share` is needed from here on.
- Back up `share` and its passphrase separately. The share file is useless without the
  passphrase, and the passphrase is useless without the file.

**7. Publish.** `ceremony.json`, `round1/` and `group.json` are public and should be published
with the network's configuration, so anyone can run the audit. `round2/` is ciphertext and can
be discarded.

## Proving the vault before it holds anything

Before the vault address goes into a rollup config:

1. Send a small amount to it from any wallet.
2. Start the signers and a bridge against a rehearsal rollup whose vault is this address
   (below). The bridge's first act is to receive that send, which needs a quorum signature.
3. Stop members one at a time: with *t* up, it keeps working; with *t* − 1, it stops and says so.

## Running the signers

Every member runs, on their own machine:

```sh
zvm committee signer -group group.json -share share -passphrase-file pass -token-file token \
  -config rollup.json -l1 http://<your NoM node>:35997 -eth http://<your ZVM node>:8545 \
  -db signer.db -listen :8601 -payout-depth 30
```

**A signer must read its own NoM node and its own ZVM node.** Its policy is the committee's
only protection against a compromised coordinator, and a policy checked against the
coordinator's nodes checks nothing. The policy signs exactly:

- a **receive** of a send whose recipient is the vault, and
- a **payout** tagged `ZVMW ‖ id` that matches `ZVMBridge.withdrawal(id)` in *finalized* ZVM
  state — recipient, token and amount — that is not already paid, whose momentum is
  `-payout-depth` below NoM's frontier, and
- only the next block on the vault's chain, and never a second payout for the same withdrawal
  that could still land (`signed_payouts` in `signer.db` is that record — back it up with the
  share).

Anything else is refused with the reason, and logged.

The coordinator is the bridge, started with the group file and the signer URLs:

```sh
zvm bridge -config rollup.json -group group.json -signers https://a:8601,https://b:8601,... -signer-token-file token
```

It holds no share. Transport between bridge and signers is a bearer token over HTTP: put it
inside WireGuard or behind TLS with client certificates on anything but a devnet.

## The local rehearsal

This is how D15 was validated on 2026-09-15, and how to repeat it:

```sh
# a 2-of-3 ceremony, three members on one machine
mkdir -p committee/devnet && cd committee/devnet
zvm committee new -l1-chain-id 70 -threshold 2 -names alice,bob,carol -out ceremony.json
# for i in 1 2 3, in p$i/ with its own pass file: round1, round2 (twice), finish, as above
zvm committee audit -ceremony ceremony.json -round1 round1 -group group.json

# a second rollup on the same L1, vaulted by the committee, with its own inbox
zvm l2-genesis -out l2 -l1 http://127.0.0.1:45997 -start <frontier+1> \
  -evm karst -min-base-fee 1000000 -inbox-tag zvmcttee -vault <group address> \
  -bridge-fee-bps 25 -bridge-min-fee ZNN:1000000,QSR:10000000

# the vault needs plasma before it can publish a single receive, and nobody
# here can sign for it — `fund` fuses for a beneficiary, which needs no
# signature from the beneficiary
zvm fund -l1 http://127.0.0.1:45997 -to <group address> -fuse 2000

head -c 48 /dev/urandom | base64 > token
cd ../.. && docker compose -f docker-compose.yml -f docker-compose.committee.yml --profile committee up -d
```

`docker-compose.committee.yml` starts reth-c, node-c, three signers and bridge-c
(status on `:47568`). Each signer mounts only its own member directory. The rehearsal
shares one NoM node and one ZVM node among all signers, which is exactly what a real
committee must not do.

The `committee5` profile adds signer-4 and signer-5 for the resharing rehearsal
below. The bridge's signer list must match the profile that is actually up —
`NewQuorum` calls `/info` on every URL and fails if one is unreachable — so
five members means `ZVM_COMMITTEE_SIGNERS=http://signer-1:8601,…,http://signer-5:8601`.

## Changing the membership without changing the vault

A second ceremony produces a different group key, which is a different Zenon
address: every user's deposit address moves, and the rollup config changes at an
activation height. **Resharing** avoids all of that. A quorum of current members
deals a fresh sharing of the *same* secret to the new member set, at a new
threshold, and the group key — so the vault's address, and the verification of
every signature it ever made — is unchanged.

The arithmetic is in `internal/committee/reshare.go`: each dealer contributes
`λᵢ·sᵢ` (its share times its Lagrange coefficient over the dealing quorum), and
those sum to the secret. What makes it safe rather than merely convenient is
that **a dealer cannot deal anything but its own share**: its constant-term
commitment must equal λᵢ times the verification share the old group already
published, every recipient checks that before accepting a value, and the sum of
the accepted constant terms is checked against the old group key. A dealer that
substitutes a value is rejected by everyone, and a quorum cannot quietly
reshare a different vault.

The procedure:

1. Agree the **dealing quorum** (at least the old threshold) and the **incoming
   set and threshold**. Every dealer must use the same lists, or the
   coefficients differ and the result is refused rather than silently wrong.
2. Each dealer runs `Reshare`, publishes its `ReshareDeal` (commitments only)
   and sends each incoming member its evaluated value over the same sealed
   channel the ceremony's round 2 uses.
3. Each incoming member runs `ReshareFinish`, which verifies every deal against
   the old group, verifies every value against its dealer's commitments, and
   returns the new share.

As commands, with the outgoing committee's `group.json` to hand:

```sh
# any courier, against the group being preserved
zvm committee reshare plan -group group.json -quorum 1,2 -threshold 3 -members a,b,d,e,f

# every incoming member, including newcomers: publish a key to receive values at
zvm committee reshare keys -id 4 -passphrase-file pass.txt

# every dealer in the quorum
zvm committee reshare deal -id 1 -share 1.share -passphrase-file pass.txt

# every incoming member
zvm committee reshare finish -id 4 -old-group group.json -passphrase-file pass.txt
```

Each step refuses to go on if the vault would move: the plan checks its own
address against the group key, `deal` refuses a share from another committee,
and `finish` refuses a result whose address is not the one the plan named.
Run end to end on a 2-of-3 becoming a 3-of-5, all five members computed the same
group file and the address did not change.

### What the live rehearsal showed (2026-09-18, chain 70)

The whole path was exercised against a running NoM chain rather than in tests
alone: the vault took custody of seven deposits and paid four withdrawals, each
one signed by a threshold quorum and published on NoM.

| | |
|---|---|
| Deposit received, 2 of 3 | members 1+2 signed vault height 1, same hash |
| One member stopped | 2+3 formed the quorum; no operator action |
| That member restarted | rejoined and signed again; the bridge was not restarted |
| Two of three stopped | vault frozen, `no quorum of 2`, a withdrawal left unpaid |
| One brought back | the backlog — a pending receive *and* the unpaid withdrawal — cleared in seconds |
| Swap: carol out, dave in | same address, same group key, new verification shares; dave's fresh share signed |
| Expand: 2-of-3 → 3-of-5 | same address; all five shares exercised, including a 3+4+5 quorum |
| Threshold enforced after growing | two willing members could no longer move the vault: `no quorum of 3` |
| Retired member returning | rejected — `holds a share of another committee` |

That last row is worth reading precisely. The coordinator rejects a retired
member because resharing writes a **new ceremony id**, not because her key
material stopped working: the group key is byte-identical across the reshare.
Her share is still live on the old polynomial, which is exactly why step 5
exists. The check stops an *honest* stale signer from rejoining a misconfigured
committee; it is not a revocation.
4. **Prove the vault before trusting it**: the new group must reproduce the
   *existing* vault address. If it does not, stop — nothing has been lost,
   because the old shares still work.
5. Only then do the outgoing members destroy their shares. Resharing does not
   invalidate them: until they are gone, a quorum of the old set can still sign.

That last point is the operational risk and it is worth being blunt about it.
Between step 3 and step 5 there are **two** quorums that can move the vault. Keep
the window short, and treat a member who will not confirm destruction as a
member who still holds a key.

## Outflow limits

The size tiers above delay one large withdrawal. They do nothing about a
thousand small ones, which is the same drain spelled differently, so a signer
also takes a rolling cap:

```
zvm committee signer -outflow-limit ZNN:10000:24h,QSR:1000000:24h
```

"This signer will not help move more than 10,000 ZNN in any 24 hours." It is
counted from what **this member** signed, in its own journal (`signed_payouts`),
not from what the vault paid — so it holds whether or not the coordinator is
honest, and the quorum stops as soon as enough members have reached their own
limit. Wormhole's Governor pairs a per-transfer limit with a rolling daily total
for the same reason.

Members do not have to agree on the number, and there is an argument for them
not to: a committee whose members have different limits is one an attacker has
to model individually.

## What is not built

- **Hardware isolation.** Shares are passphrase-sealed files. HSMs cannot run FROST's round 2
  natively; the practical upgrade is a signer on a dedicated machine with the share on an
  encrypted volume.
- **An external audit** of `internal/committee`. The signing core matches RFC 9591's test
  vector; the DKG follows the published scheme and is tested for forged proofs, replay across
  ceremonies and cheating dealers; resharing is tested as above. None of it has been reviewed
  by anyone outside this tree.
